PART (a)  k-means on the original data D

What was done: sklearn KMeans(n_clusters=15, n_init=5, random_state=558) fitted on D.
Formula:  cost(D,B) = sum_i min_{b in B} ||D_i - b||_2^2, computed by assigning every row of
          D to its nearest centre and adding squared distances (own routine, float64).

==============================================================================
PART (b)  k-means on E = D A^T, centres B_hat = mean of ORIGINAL rows per cluster
==============================================================================
What was done: for every m in {5,10,15,20,25} and each of 5 repetitions a fresh A (m x d,
iid N(0,1/m) from numpy) was drawn, E = D A^T formed, KMeans(15, n_init=5) run on E, the
clusters kept and centres rebuilt in R^d as  b_hat_j = (1/|C_j|) sum_{i in C_j} D_i.
Formulas: cost(D,B_hat) = sum_i min_j ||D_i - b_hat_j||^2 ; ratio = cost(D,B_hat)/cost(D,B).
Why: centres from E live in R^m and cannot be compared with rows of D, but the CLUSTERS
carry over; the mean of a cluster is the best centre for that cluster, so B_hat is the natural
way to turn a clustering found in low dimension into centres in the original space.

-- Comment -------------------------------------------------------------------
* Trend with m: larger m preserves distances better (JL: relative error ~ sqrt(1/m)), so the
  projected clustering is closer to the clustering found in R^d: the mean ratio is expected
  to approach 1 and the spread to shrink. Observed: mean 1.2696 -> 1.0063, spread 0.4236 -> 0.0554.
  (Small m can still give ratios close to 1 here because the 15 clusters are very well
  separated in KDD: attack types such as smurf/neptune form tight, far-apart groups.)
* Ratios below 1 (7 runs): (m=10, rep=3): 0.97582, (m=15, rep=2): 0.99515, (m=15, rep=3): 0.98152, (m=20, rep=0): 0.97782, (m=20, rep=1): 0.98347, (m=25, rep=1): 0.98898, (m=25, rep=2): 0.97260
  A ratio < 1 does NOT contradict JL. The baseline B is only a local optimum of Lloyd's
  algorithm (k-means is NP-hard; n_init=5 does not guarantee the global minimum), so
  cost(D,B) >= OPT. A clustering found on the projected data can land in a different,
  better local optimum and, after recomputing the exact means in R^d, beat B. JL only
  promises cost(D,B_hat) <= (1+eps) * OPT, and OPT <= cost(D,B).

==============================================================================
PART (c)  Centres mapped back with the pseudo-inverse: B+ = {A^+ a}
==============================================================================
What was done: in the same 25 runs each centre a in R^m found by k-means on E was mapped
to R^d by A^+ a, A^+ = numpy.linalg.pinv(A) (d x m). cost(D,B+) is evaluated exactly like
cost(D,B) (nearest-centre assignment over the rows of D).
Formula: ratio_+ = cost(D,B+)/cost(D,B).
Why: B+ needs only the m-dim centres, no second pass over the clusters; we test whether
that cheaper route is as good as recomputing means from the original rows (B_hat).

-- Comment / reasoning -------------------------------------------------------
* B_hat consists of the actual cluster means in R^d, i.e. the optimal centres for the partition
  found, so its cost is the best any centre set can do for that partition.
* B+ = A^+ a lies in the m-dimensional row space of A and has no component along the other
  d-m directions in which the data D_i do vary; every row pays for that missing part, and
  A^+ A is only a projection, so B+ is not an approximation of the cluster means in R^d.
* B_hat costs one extra pass over D (O(nd)); computing pinv(A) costs O(d m^2). So B_hat is
  both better and cheap. Use B_hat.

What was done: in every run of (b) the wall-clock time of 'draw A, compute E = D A^T, run

reducing d to m cuts this to O(n k m), while forming E costs O(n d m).

==============================================================================
PART (e)  Distortion of single pair distances vs distortion of the k-means cost
==============================================================================
What was done: 10000 random pairs (i,l) with D_i != D_l were drawn once (same pairs for every m).
(i)  For ONE draw of A per m: r_pair = ||E_i - E_l||^2 / ||D_i - D_l||^2 for all pairs.
(ii) For 20 independent draws of A per m: r_cost = cost_E(C_D) / cost_D(C_D), where C_D is the
     clustering from (a) and cost_X(C) = sum_j sum_{i in C_j} ||X_i - mu_j(X)||^2 with mu_j the
     cluster mean in X (computed as sum||x||^2 - ||sum x||^2/n_j, in float64).
     cost_D(C_D) = 53618.7759
Why: JL says E||A x||^2 = ||x||^2, with ||A x||^2/||x||^2 ~ chi^2_m / m for a FIXED x, so
a single pair has relative std sqrt(2/m). The k-means cost for a fixed partition is a sum of
many squared distances (it equals sum_j 1/(2 n_j) sum_{i,l in C_j} ||X_i - X_l||^2), so errors of
different pairs partly cancel and its ratio should be much more concentrated.

#  Smaller spread: the cost ratio r_cost (ii)  (std smaller than the pairwise ratio's std for 5 of 5 values of m).
#  Change with m: std of pair ratio 0.4296 (m=5) -> 0.2254 (m=25), close to sqrt(2/m) = 0.6325 -> 0.2828.
#                  std of cost ratio 0.1758 (m=5) -> 0.0484 (m=25).
#  Both are centred near 1 (JL is unbiased) and both shrink as m grows.
##############################################################################

-- Comment -------------------------------------------------------------------
* Pairwise ratios: each pair is distorted like chi^2_m/m, so the box is wide (std ~ sqrt(2/m))
  and has a long upper tail for small m; it falls like 1/sqrt(m).
* Cost ratio: an average over very many pairs inside clusters, so over-/under-stretched
  pairs cancel; boxes are much narrower, and still narrow further as m grows. This is why
  JL can preserve the k-means cost with a small m even though single distances are noisy.
